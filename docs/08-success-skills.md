# Success Skills

Five skills. **You pick one per project**, with one to three dimensions inside it.

Full definitions, dimensions, and four performance levels each: [`framework/success-skills/`](../framework/success-skills/). Human-readable rubrics: [`rubrics/success-skills/`](../rubrics/success-skills/).

---

## Why one and not five

Every project involves all five. That is not the same as teaching one.

Teaching a skill explicitly means: naming it, showing what it looks like, giving a routine for practising it, having the learner reflect on their own use of it, and assessing it separately from the content. That takes real time. You have that time once per project, not five times.

Name five skills and you'll teach none — you'll assess five and grow none. It is the most frequently broken constraint in the framework and the most consequential, which is why the schema makes a second skill structurally impossible rather than merely discouraged.

**One skill. One to three dimensions. Chosen for a gap you have actually observed.**

## The five

### Critical Thinking

Making reasoned judgments — working out what evidence means, drawing conclusions the evidence supports, evaluating the quality of claims including your own.

**Dimensions:** explanation-and-analysis · inference-and-evidence · evaluation-of-claims · problem-framing

**Needs from the project:** a driving question with genuinely competing answers, sources that disagree, a product that requires justifying a position, an audience who might push back.

**Don't pick it if** the answer is known in advance. No amount of good design elsewhere recovers from that.

→ [critical-thinking.yaml](../framework/success-skills/critical-thinking.yaml)

---

### Self-Directed Learning

Setting goals, getting started, keeping going when it's hard, noticing when you're off track, owning the outcome.

**Dimensions:** self-regulation · persistence · autonomy · responsibility

**Needs from the project:** genuine open space where the learner decides what happens next, enough duration that a week-two decision has week-three consequences, learner-owned management tools, and **an adult who is deliberately not always available.**

**The one-learner paradox:** this is the most commonly chosen skill in homeschool and tutoring settings, and the hardest to grow there — because you're always there. If you answer every question immediately you cannot assess self-direction; you've removed the conditions for it. Deliberate unavailability is an instructional choice, not neglect.

→ [self-directed-learning.yaml](../framework/success-skills/self-directed-learning.yaml)

---

### Complex Communication

Understanding what others are actually saying, and making yourself understood — in words, in writing, in how you carry yourself, and with the tools you choose.

**Dimensions:** synthesis-and-clarity · audience-awareness · non-verbal · tool-use

**Needs from the project:** a real audience that isn't obliged to be interested, enough rehearsal in the calendar to actually improve, feedback separated into content-first then delivery, and content complex enough that clarity is an achievement.

**Don't pick it** and then leave the presentation as the last thing you plan. It needs three rehearsals scheduled and two separate feedback rounds.

→ [complex-communication.yaml](../framework/success-skills/complex-communication.yaml)

---

### Creativity & Innovation

Producing something both **novel** and **useful** — new relative to what this learner had seen, and genuinely fit for its purpose.

**Dimensions:** idea-generation · novelty-and-usefulness · explaining-choices · risk-and-iteration

**The misconception:** creativity is not artistic talent and it is not decoration. A learner can score highly here with a spreadsheet, a survey design, or a mechanism — and poorly with a beautiful drawing that copies its model. If your assessment reduces to "how pretty is it," you're assessing something else.

**Novelty is relative to this learner.** A nine-year-old independently reinventing a known technique is demonstrating creativity.

**Needs from the project:** several genuinely viable solutions, time and permission for at least one attempt to fail, criteria about fitness for purpose rather than polish, and real users to test usefulness against.

→ [creativity.yaml](../framework/success-skills/creativity.yaml)

---

### Collaboration

Working with other people to produce something none of you would have produced alone.

**Dimensions:** planning-and-decisions · building-on-thinking · productive-disagreement · shared-responsibility

**What's assessed** is the quality of the collaborative interaction, not the quality of the product. A team can produce excellent work through terrible collaboration — one person doing everything — and that scores low.

> ### ⚠ Read this before targeting collaboration for a solo learner
>
> Collaboration is the one skill that **cannot be faked alone.** Without a genuine second party who has their own stake and their own opinions, you'll end up assessing compliance with an adult, which is a different thing and teaches the wrong lesson.
>
> Target it only if one of these is genuinely true:
>
> - A co-op member, sibling, or partner learner is a real collaborator with shared ownership of the product and the standing to disagree
> - The learner works with an outside expert or community partner across several exchanges — not one interview
> - The learner has a client whose requirements they don't control
>
> **An adult who can overrule the learner is not a collaborator.** If none of the above holds, pick complex-communication or self-directed-learning. Both are genuinely assessable alone and both transfer.
>
> `pbl profile check` warns when you target collaboration with `work_mode: solo` in a homeschool or tutoring setting.

→ [collaboration.yaml](../framework/success-skills/collaboration.yaml)

## How to choose

**1. What have you actually observed?**
Not what they need in general — what you watched happen. "Asked why the mud collects at the bottom of the driveway, she said 'because it's the bottom' and was satisfied" points straight at explanation-and-analysis. General worries point nowhere.

**2. Does the project you're planning create the conditions?**
Each skill needs specific things from the project (listed above). If the project doesn't supply them, either change the project or change the skill. A tightly-scaffolded fixed-sequence project cannot develop self-direction; there's nothing to direct.

**3. Pick one or two dimensions, not three.**
Three is the schema maximum and usually too many. One dimension taught properly beats three mentioned.

**4. Write down how you'll teach it.**
The plan has a `taught_how` field. It's the most-skipped field, and skipping it is why success skills don't grow. One named routine, used daily, handed over to the learner by the end, is worth more than any amount of rubric language.

## The four levels

Every dimension has the same four: **beginning → emerging → developing → demonstrating.**

They describe the behaviour, not the learner. A learner is at `beginning` *on this dimension, in this context, today*. Same learner may be `developing` on a different dimension of the same skill.

**Two levels of growth in one project is a lot.** One level is a real result. If your target is `demonstrating` from a `beginning` start in four weeks, you're going to be disappointed by something that was actually fine.

---

## Assessing it

- **Separately from content.** A learner can produce an excellent product with weak critical thinking, and vice versa. Two judgments.
- **Same task, twice.** Week one and the last week, on the same prompt, so the comparison means something. Show the learner both.
- **Self-assessment first.** Against the specific rows, in their own words, before you say anything. The rubrics are for the learner as much as for you.
- **Only the named rows.** Everything else on that rubric is out of scope for this project, and saying so out loud is part of keeping the focus real.
