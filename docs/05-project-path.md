# The Project Path

Four phases carry a project from launch to culmination. All four appear in every project, including a two-week one. Only the proportions change.

Machine-readable, with cross-phase invariants: [`framework/project-path.yaml`](../framework/project-path.yaml).

```
LAUNCH  →  BUILD KNOWLEDGE  →  DEVELOP & CRITIQUE  →  PRESENT
 ~10%           ~40%                  ~35%              ~15%
```

---

## Phase 1 — Launch

**Purpose:** create a genuine need to know. The learner meets the problem *before* they have the tools, and generates the questions that drive everything after.

**They're asking:** What is this actually asking me to do? What do I already know? What do I need to find out? Who's going to see this? Why does this matter?

**You're doing:** running the [entry event](10-strategies/entry-event.md); presenting or co-constructing the driving question; capturing Knows / Need-to-Knows / Next Steps; naming the audience and the date out loud.

**Out the other end:** a posted need-to-know list and a learner who wants an answer.

**One learner:** you lose the room. Thirty kids generate momentum off each other; one does not. So the stimulus has to be stronger — go somewhere, meet someone, break something open. And write the questions down: you will both feel like you remember the conversation, and by week two the list will have quietly become your objectives instead of their questions.

The compensation: you can wait for the right moment. A class launch happens on the scheduled Monday. You can wait for the rain.

**Fails by:** announcing the project instead of launching it. If their first experience is you explaining the assignment, this phase didn't happen.

---

## Phase 2 — Build Knowledge, Understanding & Skills

**Purpose:** the learner answers their own questions and acquires what the project requires — with instruction arriving when they need it, not before.

**They're asking:** Where do I find this out? Can I trust this source? How does this connect to what I found last week? What new questions do I have?

**You're doing:** short targeted lessons tied to live need-to-knows; helping evaluate sources; keeping the [learning log](10-strategies/learning-log.md) going; checking formatively and adjusting — this is where plans change most; bringing in an [expert](10-strategies/outside-experts.md) while there's still time to use what they say.

**Out the other end:** enough knowledge to start making something real.

**One learner:** this is the phase that stalls. No peer momentum, and nobody notices a quiet day. The check-in cadence in your support plan matters more here than anywhere else. Keep lessons genuinely short — a one-learner mini-lesson is five minutes, not fifteen, because you can see comprehension immediately instead of checking across thirty people.

**Fails by:** front-loading. Teach the whole unit here and the project became dessert; inquiry died in phase one.

---

## Phase 3 — Develop & Critique

**Purpose:** the learner builds their answer, gets structured feedback on it, and revises. **Quality comes from this phase or it doesn't come at all.**

**They're asking:** Is this on the right track? What's missing? How do I make it better? Do I need to go find out more?

**You're doing:** running at least one [critique protocol](10-strategies/critique-protocols.md) against the criteria; scheduling [revision](10-strategies/revision.md) as its own block; teaching whatever the building reveals is missing; having them self-assess against the rubric before you say anything.

**Out the other end:** a revised product measurably better than its first version.

**One learner:** critique needs a second person. Decide who **before the project starts** and put them in `support_plan.critique_partners` — a sibling, a co-op, a grandparent, an online group, an expert, or you deliberately wearing a critic's hat rather than a coach's. Unnamed critique partners mean no critique.

**Fails by:** compressing critique and revision into one session, or letting the first draft be the final draft because time ran out. **When the schedule slips, protect this phase and take the time from Build Knowledge instead.** Most people do the reverse, and it's the single most common way a good project produces mediocre work.

---

## Phase 4 — Present Products

**Purpose:** the work goes to real people, the learner explains their reasoning, and then they reflect on the whole arc.

**They're asking:** What do I need to explain about this? How do I hold a room? What did I actually learn? What would I do differently?

**You're doing:** [rehearsing](10-strategies/presentations.md) — content feedback first, delivery feedback later; handling logistics so they only have to think about the work; briefing the audience and giving them a [feedback form](10-strategies/audience-feedback.md); running a real [reflection](10-strategies/post-project-reflection.md) afterwards; celebrating properly.

**Out the other end:** work delivered to a real audience, plus their reflection on it.

**One learner:** the comfort ramp lives here — one trusted adult, then a small friendly group, then the real audience, as three separate calendar events. A learner who has already said it out loud twice walks into the real thing differently.

**Fails by:** shrinking the audience at the last minute. Also: skipping reflection because the presentation felt like the end. It isn't — reflection is where the learning consolidates, and it works better two days later than the same night.

---

## What must be true across the whole path

`pbl review` enforces these. They're the difference between four labelled sections and an actual project.

| | |
|---|---|
| **All four phases present** | Even in two weeks. |
| **A critique cycle completes before Present** | Feedback that arrives with the grade is not critique. |
| **Two-plus reflection points, in two-plus phases** | Reflection only at the end is a summary. |
| **Formative assessment in both middle phases** | The only two phases where you can still change course. |
| **Every milestone carries a need-to-know question** | Otherwise instruction is pre-scheduled, not responsive. |

---

## Pacing a short project

Under four weeks, the temptation is to cut Develop & Critique. Don't — that's where quality lives. Cut here instead:

- **Narrow the product.** One thing done properly, not two done thinly.
- **Compress Build Knowledge.** Fewer sources, one expert instead of three, one field visit.
- **Keep the critique cycle.** Even a single 30-minute critique plus a 45-minute revision block is worth more than another day of research.
- **Keep the audience.** Shrink it if you must — one expert is a legitimate public audience — but don't remove it.

Under two weeks, ask honestly whether this is a project or a good activity. Both are fine; only one needs this framework.
