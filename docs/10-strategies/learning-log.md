# Learning Log

**Purpose:** Track what the learner is finding, where it came from, and what it opened up.

## Where it goes

**Build Knowledge**, primarily. Often continues into Develop & Critique.

## How to run it

Four columns:

| Question I'm answering | Source I'm using | What I learned (and date) | What I want to know now |
|---|---|---|---|

The rhythm:

1. Learner picks one need-to-know question — the one they most want answered.
2. Research for 15–20 minutes.
3. Stop. Log it. Say out loud what they found and what it opened up.
4. Go again.

Short cycles beat long sessions. The pausing is the point: it forces the "and what now?" that turns collecting into thinking.

## Why it works

It makes the research trail visible, so a claim in the final product can be traced to a source. That directly serves the standards about gathering and citing information — the log *is* the evidence.

It also catches the learner who has been reading for forty minutes without finding anything, which is otherwise invisible.

## With one learner

The share-out step needs a person. You are that person, and it should be a genuine exchange, not a check-in — ask something you don't know the answer to.

**The best adaptation is a running voice memo** for a learner who resists writing. Same four prompts, spoken, transcribed later or not at all. The thinking is the target; the handwriting isn't.

**Keep it in one notebook with the measurements and sketches.** A separate research log gets abandoned by week two. One notebook, one place, that they own.

## By grade band

- **Pre-K–2:** Three prompts, drawn as much as written: what I wonder about / what I think is true now / how I found out.
- **3–5:** Four columns work. Model the fourth column explicitly — new questions don't come naturally at first.
- **6–12:** Add a reliability column. Judging sources is a standard in its own right at this level.

## Common mistakes

- **Making it a worksheet.** If it's assessed for neatness it stops being a thinking tool.
- **Skipping the fourth column.** Then it's a note-taking form, not an inquiry record.
- **No source column.** Then in week four nobody can remember where the number came from, which is a real problem when it goes public.
- **Logging after the fact.** Wednesday's entry written on Friday is fiction.
