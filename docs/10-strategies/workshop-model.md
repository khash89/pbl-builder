# Workshop Model

**Purpose:** A repeatable session structure — short lesson, real work time, share.

## Where it goes

**Build Knowledge** and **Develop & Critique.** It's the default shape of a working session.

## How to run it

```
MINI-LESSON        10-15 min   One thing. Tied to what they need right now.
     ↓
WORK TIME          20-40 min   They work. You circulate and confer.
     ↓
SHARE              5-10 min    Progress, problems, one thing learned.
```

**Mini-lesson rules:** one topic, explicitly connected to a current need-to-know, and short. If it runs to twenty-five minutes it isn't a mini-lesson and work time just got eaten.

**Work time is theirs.** Your job is conferring — short individual conversations, questions rather than answers. Not marking, not preparing tomorrow.

**Share is not optional.** It's the accountability and it's where you find out what actually happened.

## Why the structure matters

It mirrors how skills are actually acquired outside school: learn a technique, use it on something that matters, get feedback. It also makes the day predictable, which reduces the executive-function load of figuring out what's happening.

## With one learner

The proportions change substantially.

**Mini-lesson: five minutes, not fifteen.** A class mini-lesson is padded with checking for understanding across thirty people. With one learner you can see comprehension immediately. Say the thing, check, move on.

**Work time: longer, and protect it.** This is the big win of one-on-one — no transitions, no crowd management. But it's also where the temptation to hover lives. Conferring means two or three short check-ins, not continuous presence. Consider working on your own thing nearby (the `body-double` scaffold) rather than watching.

**Share: needs a real audience.** Sharing progress to the person who watched you do it is hollow. Options: a phone message to a grandparent, a photo sent to a co-op group, or a written entry that gets read tomorrow rather than now. If none of those work, replace share with a written self-report they read back to themselves next session — that at least serves self-monitoring.

**Don't run it every day.** With one learner it becomes claustrophobic. Two or three workshop days a week, with the others being long unstructured build sessions, works better.

## By grade band

- **Pre-K–2:** Ten-minute work blocks. Mini-lesson under five. Expect two cycles per session, not one.
- **3–5:** The standard shape works. Build the share-out habit early.
- **6–12:** Can handle a 90-minute block with one mini-lesson and a mid-point reconnect. They can also run the share themselves.

## Common mistakes

- **The mini-lesson expanding.** It always wants to. Set a timer.
- **Teaching something they don't need yet.** The lesson must answer a live question.
- **Skipping the share because time ran out.** Then nobody knows what happened, including them.
- **Running it identically forever.** Predictable structure, varied content — not both fixed.
