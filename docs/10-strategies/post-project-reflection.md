# Post-Project Reflection

**Purpose:** Learn from the project — for the learner, and for whoever designs the next one.

Two separate reflections. The learner's, and yours. Both get skipped, and yours is the one that compounds.

## Where it goes

**Present**, after the public product. **Not the same day.**

Wait two days. On the night of a presentation the learner is flooded and will say "good" and "fine." Two days later you get the honest version, and it's a completely different conversation.

## The learner's reflection

- What were the main steps of this project? *(Reconstructing the arc is itself the exercise.)*
- What's the most important thing you learned?
- What would you do differently?
- Where did you do your best work?
- What was the worst part?
- How could I have made this better for you?

That last one matters. Ask it seriously, and don't defend the answer.

**"I used to think… now I think"** is the best closing structure there is — on the content, on the process, and on themselves. That third one is often the real outcome: *"I used to think I was bad at reading. Now I think I'm slow at reading."*

## Your reflection

Plus/Delta, honestly, within a week:

| Plus — what worked | Delta — what to change |
|---|---|
| Which parts genuinely landed? | Where did it stall, and why? |
| What surprised you? | What did you have to rescue? |
| What did they do that you didn't expect? | What would you cut? |

Then check the plan against two rubrics — [project-design-rubric.md](../../rubrics/project-design-rubric.md) for the design, [project-based-teaching-rubric.md](../../rubrics/project-based-teaching-rubric.md) for how you ran it. Write it in the plan's **Notes & Adaptations** section while it's fresh. In three months you will not remember why week two went badly.

## Then update the profile

This is the step that makes the next project better, and almost nobody does it.

The learner profile is now out of date in specific, useful ways:

- `pbl_experience` moves from `none` to `some`
- `success_skill_target.current_level` — did the targeted dimension move? Evidence?
- `standards_targets[].status` — which are now secure?
- `preferences.audience_comfort` — after a real audience, often shifts
- `preferences.choice_appetite` — did they take more choice than you offered?
- `interests` — new ones appear during a project. Write them down; they're next project's topic.
- `needs` — which scaffolds faded successfully? Those needs may be resolved.

An updated profile is the actual deliverable of a finished project, beyond the product itself.

## And celebrate

Properly, and separately from the assessment. Whatever form fits. This is not sentimentality — a project that ends in a debrief and nothing else teaches that finishing hard things is unremarkable.

## With one learner

**Do it in motion.** A walk, a drive, doing dishes. Sitting across a table asking a child to evaluate a project you designed together is a strange social situation and produces performed answers.

**You have to invite the criticism explicitly**, and mean it. In a class, negative feedback arrives anonymously and in volume. Here, the learner has to say it to your face, and if they think it'll hurt you they won't. Say: *"Tell me the worst part. I'd rather know."* Then don't flinch.

**Your reflection matters more here.** A class teacher runs the same project with a new cohort and gets a natural second attempt. You get one shot per learner, so the write-up is your only compounding asset.

**Record the abandonment.** If the project didn't finish, write down why and set `status: abandoned` in the plan. That's a legitimate outcome and the note is worth more than a plan that quietly stopped being mentioned.

## By grade band

- **Pre-K–2:** Two questions, verbal, with drawing. "What was your favourite part? What was hard?"
- **3–5:** Four or five questions, written or spoken. They can identify their own best work.
- **6–12:** Full reflection with written self-assessment against the rubric, plus a genuine critique of the project design.

## Common mistakes

- **Same day.** You get performance, not reflection.
- **Skipping yours.** The design mistake repeats on the next project.
- **Not updating the profile.** The single highest-value thing here, and the most skipped.
- **Defending the project when they criticise it.** Ends the honesty permanently.
- **Assessment instead of reflection.** Related, different. Do both, separately.
- **No celebration.** Or a celebration fused with the grade.
