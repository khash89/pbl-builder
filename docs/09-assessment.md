# Assessment in PBL

Two things get assessed, separately: **the content** and **the one success skill.** A learner can produce an excellent product with weak critical thinking, and the reverse. Collapsing them into one judgment loses both.

---

## Formative assessment

The point is not to record where they are. It's to **change what you do next.**

Which is why the plan schema requires a `responds_how` on every formative check. Assessment you don't act on is record-keeping.

**Required in both middle phases** — Build Knowledge and Develop & Critique — because those are the only two phases where you can still change course. `pbl review` fails a plan missing either.

Tools that work:

| Tool | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| [Need-to-know list](10-strategies/need-to-know.md) | What they think the problem is |
| [Learning log](10-strategies/learning-log.md) | Whether research is producing anything |
| Exit ticket | What landed in this session |
| Rubric self-check | Whether they can see their own gaps |
| Conferring during work time | Almost everything, and it's free |
| A specific counted behaviour | Movement on the target skill |

That last one deserves emphasis. Pick one observable thing and count it weekly — links in a reasoning chain, questions asked unprompted, sessions started without a card. A number you track beats an impression you form.

---

## Summative assessment

- **The product, against criteria derived from the standards.** Not from neatness. The test: could they meet every criterion and still not have learned the standard?
- **The public presentation.** Audience feedback informs your judgment; it isn't the judgment.
- **At least one outside voice**, if you can get one. See below.

---

## Self-assessment

In a one-learner setting this carries more weight than in a classroom, so it has to be taught rather than assumed.

**The learner goes first. Always.** They score against the criteria and justify each score *before* you say anything. If you go first, you've replaced their judgment with yours and learned nothing about whether they can see their own work.

Then compare. The gap between their assessment and yours is itself the most useful data in the project — a learner who consistently overrates or underrates their work has told you something more important than the score.

## What changes with one learner

### Individual accountability stops being a problem

The hardest assessment problem in classroom PBL — did *this* learner actually contribute? — simply evaporates. Everything is theirs.

### Objectivity becomes the problem instead

You are assessing someone you love, whose effort you watched, whose frustration you sat through. That is a genuinely difficult position and pretending otherwise doesn't help.

Three defences:

**1. Written criteria that existed before the draft.** Score against the words, not against the effort you witnessed. This is the main one.

**2. Separate effort from achievement.** Both matter. Record them as two things. "She worked extraordinarily hard and the model doesn't meet criterion 3" is a coherent, useful, honest statement. Merging them makes both unreadable.

**3. Get one outside voice on the final product.** An expert, a co-op teacher, an audience member with the criteria in hand. Not because your judgment is bad, but because a second reading is the only real check available to you.

### There's no distribution to hide in

A class teacher can say a piece of work is "about average for this group." You have no group. Which means the criteria have to be absolute — anchored to the standard, or to [exemplars](10-strategies/models-of-quality-work.md), not to a cohort.

This is more honest, and harder. Exemplars are the practical answer: three real examples pinned up, and the question "which of these is yours closest to?"

### Grades, if you need them

Many homeschool and microschool settings need something for a transcript. Two notes:

- The rubric is what generates the grade, and the learner should have had it from day one.
- Grade the work, not the growth. Then record the growth separately, in the profile — that's where it's actually useful, because it's what the next project is designed from.

## The reflection exception

**Do not grade reflections.** A graded reflection immediately becomes a performance: the learner writes what you want to read, and you lose the only honest channel you had.

Respond instead. Affirm the realisation, ask one probing question, move on. → [Reflection](10-strategies/reflection.md)

## Assessing the one success skill

- **Same task, twice.** First week and last week, same prompt. Count the same thing both times. Show them the comparison — it's the most motivating artifact in the project.
- **Only the named dimensions.** Everything else on the rubric is out of scope. Say so.
- **One level of growth in a project is a real result.** Two is a lot. If you're expecting `beginning` to `demonstrating` in four weeks you'll be disappointed by something that went fine.

## What to record when it's over

The finished plan's **Notes & Adaptations** section, plus an updated [learner profile](15-learner-profiles.md). The profile update is the part almost everyone skips and it's what makes the next project better:

- `pbl_experience` — probably moved
- `success_skill_target.current_level` — did the dimension move? What's the evidence?
- `standards_targets[].status` — which are now secure?
- `audience_comfort`, `choice_appetite` — often shift after a real audience
- `interests` — new ones surface during projects. They're the next project's topic.
- `needs` — which scaffolds faded successfully?

An updated profile is the second deliverable of a finished project.

---

## Common mistakes

- **Criteria about presentation rather than learning.** Neat and wrong scores well.
- **You self-assess first.** Replaces their judgment with yours.
- **Formative assessment you don't act on.** Then it's a diary.
- **Grading reflections.** Ends honesty.
- **Merging effort and achievement.** Makes both unreadable.
- **Assessing all five success skills.** One, with named rows.
- **No outside voice at all.** The single most valuable check available in a solo setting.
- **Not updating the profile.** Loses the compounding.
