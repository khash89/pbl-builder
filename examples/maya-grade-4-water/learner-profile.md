# Learner Profile: Maya

## What Lights Them Up

Water moving through dirt. That's the whole thing. After any real rain she is
outside for as long as we let her, digging channels off the side of the driveway
and rerouting the runoff — dams, spillways, little diverted streams. She has been
doing this since she was five and has not got bored of it once. She narrates it
while she works.

The interest is specifically about *control and consequence*, not about water as
a topic. She isn't interested in reading about rivers. She is interested in what
happens downstream when she moves one shovelful. If you gave her a book about
watersheds she'd put it down; if you asked her whether she could stop the gravel
washing into the ditch, she'd already be outside.

Second thing: she is a collector and a cataloguer. Two years of creek animal
sightings with dates. She likes knowing all of something.

## What Gets Hard

**Starting.** Not the work — the starting. She'll circle a task for twenty
minutes. Once she's physically begun she's fine and often hard to stop. If I give
her the first physical action ("go get the trowel and the bucket") she starts
immediately. If I say "let's work on your project" she doesn't.

**Reading.** She has decided she's bad at it, which is now a bigger problem than
the actual gap. She reads about two years behind and slowly, but her
comprehension is fine — read the same passage aloud and she'll discuss it well.
So the block is decoding plus the belief. Any project that fronts a lot of
independent reading will fail, not because she can't, but because she'll conclude
in week one that it's a reading project and therefore not hers.

**Being watched mid-work.** She hates showing anything unfinished. This matters
for critique: she'll need it framed as normal and expected before the first
draft exists, not sprung on her.

**Audiences.** She goes quiet, not chatty, when nervous. She has never presented
to anyone outside the four of us. She isn't dramatic about it, she just
disappears. I don't want to skip this — I want it built up gradually.

## What We've Already Tried

A unit on the water cycle last year from a boxed curriculum. It went badly. It was
almost all reading and worksheets, the diagrams were of a generic mountain and a
generic cloud, and she never once connected it to the creek she can see from the
kitchen. She finished it and retained nothing. That's the failure I'm trying not
to repeat: correct content, no contact with anything real, delivered in her
weakest channel.

Things that have worked: anything where she makes a physical thing first and we
name it afterwards. She learned what a siphon was by accidentally making one.

Already ruled out: a written report. A lapbook. Anything where the output is
mostly text — she'll produce it, badly, and hate it, and we'll have taught her
again that school is the place her weakness shows.
