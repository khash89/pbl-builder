# Where Does Our Water Go?

## Project Summary

Maya spends every rainy afternoon digging channels in the driveway mud and
rerouting the runoff. Over four weeks she turns that into a real investigation:
she marks six stakes on the eroding driveway, measures how much gravel the rain
takes away, tests three of her own designs for slowing it down under identical
conditions in a runoff table, and recommends the one the measurements support.
She builds a tabletop model of the driveway showing the problem and her fix side
by side, and demonstrates it — with a jug of water — to the watershed council at
the library.

## Why This Project For This Learner

**The interest needed no manufacturing.** Maya already does this. She has been
digging dams in the runoff since she was five and hasn't got bored once. The only
new elements are measurement and the requirement to prove which dam is best. Her
mother's stated goal was for her to discover that the thing she does for fun is a
field adults get paid to think about, and that only works if the project is
recognisably the same activity.

**Everything routes around reading without lowering the demand.** Maya reads two
years behind and, more importantly, has decided she is bad at it. The boxed water
cycle unit last year failed precisely because it was reading-first — she concluded
in week one that it was a reading project and therefore not hers. So the evidence
here comes from her own stakes and her own tests, the sources are tiered with her
choosing the entry point, and one of the three is deliberately an adult-level
handout read aloud together. That last one matters: it lets her be the person who
understands the hardest document in the room, with support, rather than the person
who gets the easy version.

**The product presents for her.** Her audience comfort is low and she goes silent
rather than chatty when nervous. A physical model means the council will be
looking at a tray of water, not at a nine-year-old. She still has to do a
five-minute walk-through and take questions — the demand is intact — but the
object carries it. Building things is also her strongest channel and one of her
three product modes, and I gave her four low-pressure options and let her choose;
she picked the model over the picture book and the PSA.

**The audience is ramped, not removed.** Four rungs: grandmother, grandmother plus
brother, the coordinator on video, then the council. She has never presented to
anyone outside the family, so the ramp is longer than it will need to be next
time. The backup plan brings the coordinator to the house rather than retreating
to presenting to us — the point of the public product is a person who is not
obliged to be interested, and that survives the meeting being cancelled.

**The success skill is one row, chosen for a specific observed gap.** Critical
thinking, explanation-and-analysis only. She reports accurately and stops at the
first link: asked why mud collects at the bottom of the driveway, she said
"because it's the bottom" and was satisfied. So the whole skill intervention is
one routine — "and then what?" — run daily, with a written three-link minimum, and
handed over to her by week 3. In week 3 she has to find a hole in her own
explanation, which is the beginning of the next level up. Nothing else on the
critical thinking rubric is being assessed and that is deliberate.

**What I made hard on purpose.** She commits to her first idea and defends it.
That's why three designs must be tested under identical conditions and all three
must appear on the comparison page including the two that lost. Her choice
appetite is medium, not low — she doesn't freeze, she over-commits — so the
three-design constraint is there to force comparison, not to reduce anxiety, and
it comes off after the first test.

**The tripwire is real.** If she hasn't voluntarily gone outside to work on this
by the end of week 2, it hasn't caught, and the plan says to shrink it rather than
push. That's in the file because week-2 me will want to push.

## Week-by-Week Plan

Six hours a week, two-hour weekday mornings. There is deliberate slack: the
project needs two rain events and the weather does not take instruction. If the
rain doesn't come, the runoff table substitutes and the real-driveway measurement
slides.

### Week 1 — Launch

| Day | What happens | Ready when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Walk the runoff in the rain. Draw it from memory. Name the project after the drawing. | The drawing exists and she has asked at least two questions |
| 2 | Set and number six stakes at the spots *she* picks. Fixed-position photos. Baseline measurements. | Six stakes, six photos, six numbers in the notebook |
| 3 | "And then what?" introduced on her own drawing. First chain written as arrows. Vocabulary named after the experience. | One chain of three or more links |

*If it doesn't rain in week 1, day 1 becomes hose-on-the-driveway and we wait for
real rain to arrive whenever it arrives.*

### Week 2 — Build Knowledge

| Day | What happens | Ready when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Second measurement round after rain. Calculate change at each stake. | Two rounds compared, in her own numbers |
| 2 | Three tiered sources, her choice of entry point. Sort notes into 'how it works' / 'what it costs'. | Notes categorised, not copied in order |
| 3 | Video call with the coordinator. Her questions from her notebook. I stay quiet. | Answers written in the notebook, plus at least one new question |
| 4 | Look at the two erosion-control installations on the town road. What is each one doing? | She can say what each installation is for |
| 5 | Build the runoff table. Write the constants on it in marker. One no-intervention practice run. | A table that behaves like the driveway |

**Midweek check:** are the source notes categorised? Fix it here or it compounds.

### Week 3 — Develop & Critique

| Day | What happens | Ready when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Test designs 1 and 2. Same pour, same slope. Photograph and measure. | Two results recorded, including the disappointing one |
| 2 | Test design 3. Complete the comparison page — all three, including the losers. | Comparison page shows three designs and a winner |
| 3 | She runs the chain herself on the winning design. Find-the-hole task. | Three links unprompted, and one real gap identified |
| 4 | **Grandmother critique** at the model, criteria read aloud one at a time. Framed the day before. | Two unclear things written down, by Maya |
