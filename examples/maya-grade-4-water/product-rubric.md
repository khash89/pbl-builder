## The driveway model

| Criterion | From | Not yet | Getting there | Meets it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. It's OUR driveway** — slope and problem spot match her measurements, not a generic hill | 4-ESS2-1 | Generic slope; no relationship to the stakes | Roughly matches; one or two measurements reflected | Slope and the worst spot match her stake measurements; she can point at the real gully and the model gully and they correspond |
| **2. Water behaves like the real thing** | 4-ESS2-1 | Water poured through does something unrelated to the real runoff | Water runs somewhere plausible | The model gully forms where the real gully is, repeatably |

| **5. The recommendation is the one the tests supported** | 4-ESS3-2 | Recommends the design she liked before testing | Recommends the winner but can't say which test decided it | Names the test that decided it, and what it cost |

## Check on the criteria themselves

**Could Maya meet all nine and still not have learned the standards?**

No — 1, 2, and 6 require the actual observation and measurement that `4-ESS2-1`
asks for; 3, 5, and 9 require the generate-and-compare that `4-ESS3-2` asks for;
7 and 8 are `W.4.8` almost verbatim. Criterion 4 is the success skill and is
assessed separately.

The risk criterion is **2** — it could be met by a well-built model without much
understanding. It's paired with 4 for that reason: the model has to work *and*
she has to explain why.
