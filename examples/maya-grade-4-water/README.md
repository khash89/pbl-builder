# Maya · Grade 4 · "Where Does Our Water Go?"

> *How can we stop our driveway from washing into the creek?*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grade** | 4 |
| **Setting** | Homeschool, rural |
| **Subjects** | Science, English Language Arts |
| **Duration** | 4 weeks × 6 hours |
| **Target skill** | Critical thinking — `explanation-and-analysis` only |
| **Product** | Physical model of the driveway, plus a field notebook |
| **Audience** | The watershed council's monthly meeting at the library |

## What this example demonstrates

**Designing around a learner who has decided she's bad at reading — without lowering the demand.** Maya reads about two years below grade level, and the more serious problem is that she's concluded she's bad at it. A boxed water-cycle unit failed last year precisely because it was reading-first: she decided in week one it was a reading project and therefore not hers.

So the evidence here comes from six measurement stakes she placed herself. Sources are tiered with *her* choosing the entry point. And one of the three is deliberately an adult-level volunteer-monitoring handout, read aloud together — which lets her be the person who understands the hardest document available, with support, instead of the person who always gets the easy version.

**A comfort ramp instead of dropping the audience.** Her audience comfort is low and she goes silent rather than chatty when nervous. Four rungs: grandmother at the model → grandmother and brother, timed → the watershed coordinator on a video call → the council meeting. The backup plan brings the coordinator to the house rather than retreating to presenting to her parents.

**A product that presents for her.** A physical model means the council looks at a tray of water, not at a nine-year-old. The five-minute walk-through and the questions are still required — the object just carries it.

**One rubric row, chosen from one observed moment.** Asked why mud collects at the bottom of the driveway she said "because it's the bottom" and was satisfied. That's the entire basis for targeting `explanation-and-analysis`, and the whole intervention is one routine — "and then what?" — run daily with a written three-link minimum and handed over to her by week 3.

**Scaffolds that fade on triggers, not dates.** And one that explicitly doesn't fade: text-to-speech is listed in her profile's `accommodations`, not in the plan's `scaffolds`, because it shouldn't come off.

## How to adapt it

- **No creek?** Any water that goes somewhere inconvenient works — a downspout, a flooding corner of a yard, a school car park. The element that matters is that she can see it and reach it repeatedly.
- **Different subject entirely?** The transferable structure is: something she already does for fun, plus measurement, plus a real decision at the end.
- **No watershed council?** A facilities manager, a neighbour who has the same problem, a garden club, the person who maintains the road. One interested adult who isn't family is a legitimate public audience.
- **Shorter?** Cut to one measurement round and two tested designs. Keep the critique cycle and the audience.
