# Devon · Grade 8 · "The Lot on Main Street"

> *What should our town do with the empty lot on Main Street, and who actually gets to decide?*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grade** | 8 |
| **Setting** | Microschool, small town |
| **Subjects** | Social Studies, Mathematics, English Language Arts |
| **Duration** | 6 weeks × 8 hours |
| **Target skill** | Complex communication — `audience-awareness` only |
| **Product** | Policy proposal + 3-minute public comment; "who decides" map |
| **Audience** | The town council's public comment period |

## What this example demonstrates

**Turning a "difficult" trait into the engine of the project.** Devon argues constantly and enjoys it. He also doesn't read the room at all — in a co-op debate he had the best evidence and lost, because he used one escalating register on everybody and interrupted the two people who were coming round to his side. Afterwards he was certain they'd been unreasonable.

The intervention is structural rather than exhortative: **he may not state a recommendation until three residents have each confirmed he can state their position accurately.** He writes each position in their words and takes it back; if they say "that's not quite it," he goes again. Then in week 5 he argues the position he likes least, to a peer, well enough that she can't tell it isn't his.

**A hard external constraint doing the teaching.** The public comment period is three minutes and going over isn't permitted. That does more for audience awareness than any amount of instruction.

**Maths integrated without being bolted on.** `7.RP.A.3` is a grade-7 standard he's still shaky on — and the reason is that he can run the procedure and doesn't recognise when a situation calls for it. Costing three land uses on the same per-square-foot basis is unmistakably such a situation. It also routes through the one place he calculates voluntarily: he tracks the corner shop's prices and which ones moved.

**Designing around a writing aversion without lowering the demand.** A persuasive-essay unit last year produced a good argument in forty minutes of dictation and then lost three weeks to typing it. Here four written pages exist *in service of* three spoken minutes, dictated then edited, with a fade target of one section written directly.

**A sealed prediction.** His position, written in week 1 and opened in week 5. If nothing moved, that's the project's real finding — and the plan says to name it rather than celebrate a polished proposal. It's in `when_to_stop`.

**The adult as declared opposition.** Devon knows the arrangement. The teacher argues with his positions and attacks his numbers, and has told him honestly that she doesn't know what he'll conclude.

## How to adapt it

- **No empty lot?** Any live local disagreement with a decision-making body: a bus route, a library's opening hours, a proposed fee, a park's dog policy. The requirements are a real body, a real disagreement, and public minutes.
- **No public comment period?** A letter to the council that gets a reply, a school board, a housing association, a parish council. Anything with a formal channel and a time limit.
- **Only one learner, no Priya?** The critique partner needs to be someone with a genuinely different position — a grandparent who disagrees works well. Note that if that person can overrule the learner, `collaboration` would not be assessable here; that's why the target skill is complex communication.
- **Younger?** Same structure, one stakeholder interview instead of three, and a letter instead of a spoken comment.
