# The Lot on Main Street

## Project Summary

A quarter-acre lot on Main Street has been empty for eleven years. Over six
weeks Devon works out who actually has the power to change that — reading eleven
years of council minutes, filing a public-records request, and interviewing the
town clerk — costs all three proposed uses on the same basis, and interviews
three residents who disagree with each other, taking each write-up back to its
holder to check he got it right. Only then may he form a recommendation. He
delivers it as a three-minute public comment to the town council and files the
full proposal with the clerk.

## Why This Project For This Learner

**The interest is adversarial reasoning, and this is that.** Devon argues about
transfer fees with his uncle for an hour at a stretch and rates cross-examination
technique in courtroom clips. He isn't drawn to conflict; he's drawn to being
right and having shown why. A policy proposal delivered to a body that can rule
against him is that activity, in a room that matters. Nothing about the
engagement needs manufacturing.

**The writing problem is designed around, not designed out.** Last year's
persuasive-essay unit produced a genuinely good argument in forty minutes of
dictation and then lost three weeks to getting it typed. He learned nothing about
persuasion and a lot about hating school. Here the written proposal exists *in
service of* three spoken minutes — four pages supporting an argument, dictated
then edited, with a fade target of one section written directly. He can write to
speak; he cannot be made to write instead of speaking, and pretending otherwise
wastes the project.

**The success skill is one row, and the whole design points at it.** Complex
communication, audience-awareness only. The observed gap is precise: in a co-op
debate he had the best evidence in the room and lost, because he used one
escalating register on everyone and interrupted two people who were coming round.
Afterwards he was sure they'd been unreasonable — he genuinely could not see that
he'd talked them out of a position they already held.

So the intervention is structural rather than exhortative. **He may not state a
recommendation until he can state the strongest opposing view to the satisfaction
of someone who holds it.** The read-back is the mechanism: he writes each
resident's position in their words and takes it back, and if they say "that's not
quite it," he goes again. Then in week 5 he argues the position he likes least,
to Priya, well enough that she can't tell. Then every rehearsal opens with: who is
in the room and what do they already believe?

The three-minute limit does more for audience awareness than any amount of
instruction, because going over is simply not permitted.

**Maths goes where he already does maths.** He knows the corner shop's prices and
which ones moved — the only context where he calculates voluntarily. The cost
model is that, applied to a parcel of land. `7.RP.A.3` is a grade-7 standard he's
still shaky on, and the reason is that he can run the procedure and doesn't
recognise when a situation calls for it. Comparing three uses per square foot is
a situation that unmistakably calls for it.

**What I made hard on purpose.** He forms a position fast and defends it, so the
sealed week-1 prediction exists to make that visible. We open it in week 5 and
compare it to where the evidence went. If nothing moved, that's the project's real
finding and I'd rather name it than deliver a polished proposal that taught him
nothing — which is why it's also in `when_to_stop`.

**I am the opposition, openly.** He knows it. I argue with his positions and go
after his numbers, and I have told him I genuinely don't know what he'll conclude
about the lot. That's true, and it's what makes the arrangement work rather than
being a technique.

**The belief being tested is his, not mine.** He thinks civic processes are rigged
and pointless. I'm not going to argue him out of that — I'm going to have him map
the actual machinery and then use it, and find out. He may conclude he was right.
That would also be a real outcome, and better than inheriting the view untested.

## Week-by-Week Plan

Eight hours a week across four days plus Friday morning. The council calendar is
the fixed point: the comment happens on the 2nd Tuesday of week 7, and everything
else works backwards from it.
