# Learner Profile: Devon

## What Lights Them Up

Argument. Not conflict — argument. He is genuinely happy for an hour going back
and forth about whether a transfer fee was justified, and he does the reading:
he'll come back with the wage bill and the amortisation and know he's right.

The shape of the interest is **adversarial reasoning with evidence**. He likes
being right *and having shown why*. The courtroom clips are the tell — he isn't
watching for drama, he's rating technique. He'll pause and say "that was a bad
question, she gave him room."

He also has an unexpected feel for money. He knows the shop's prices and which
ones moved. It's the only context where he does arithmetic voluntarily, and he
doesn't think of it as maths.

## What Gets Hard

**He doesn't read the room, at all.** This is the thing. He has the best evidence
and loses the argument, because he uses one register — escalating — on everybody.
In the co-op debate he interrupted two people who were coming round to his side
and lost them. Afterwards he was certain they'd been unreasonable. It isn't
arrogance exactly; he seems not to register that other people arrive with
positions of their own that they might move from.

**Extended writing is a fight.** Not capability — he can construct an argument
better than most adults. It's the sitting and producing prose. He'll dictate
brilliantly and then refuse to type it. We've settled on dictate-then-edit and it
works, but a project whose main output is an essay will go badly.

**Sequencing.** He starts at the most interesting part and works outward, which
means week five arrives with the fun bits done and the foundations missing. He
needs the plan written down, by him, with dates.

**Certainty.** He forms a position fast and then defends it, which is a strength
in a debate and a problem in an investigation. He needs to be caught by evidence
at least once.

## What We've Already Tried

A persuasive-essay unit last year. He produced a genuinely good argument in about
forty minutes of dictation and then we lost three weeks to getting it written
down, revised, and formatted. He learned nothing about persuasion and quite a lot
about hating school. The content was fine; the output channel was wrong.

The co-op debate worked much better and is where the audience-awareness gap
became visible. Worth repeating the format and adding the thing that was
missing — a reason to care what the other side thinks.

**Ruled out:** anything where the primary deliverable is a long written document.
He can write in service of speaking; he cannot be made to write instead of
speaking. Also ruled out: group work where someone else does the presenting.
He'll take it over.

One caution from me: he will want to pick a side on the lot in week one and
spend five weeks assembling ammunition. The design has to make that harder than
actually finding out.
