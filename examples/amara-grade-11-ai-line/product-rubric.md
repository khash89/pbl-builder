# Product Criteria — Amara

**Products:** the data brief and recommendation (primary) · the decision log (secondary)

Amara wrote all six criteria for the brief herself in week 2, from three
published policy briefs plus one I chose because it was bad. I changed one word
in criterion 4. This is the reversal the project exists to produce: she is the
person who defines the criteria, not the person who meets them.

## The data brief

| Criterion | From | Not yet | Getting there | Meets it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The actual policy text, quoted and sourced to a dated record** | HSS-IC.B.6 | Paraphrased from what people said it says | Quoted, source unclear | Quoted verbatim with the board record and date |
| **2. Sampling decisions stated, each with its reason and the trade-off accepted** | HSS-IC.B.3 | Method described without justification | Reasons given, trade-offs implicit | Every decision has a reason and a named cost, written before distribution |
| **3. Divergence shown quantitatively, sample sizes visible** | HSS-IC.A.1 | Asserted, or a percentage with no n | Numbers with n | Divergence quantified, n visible everywhere, and the comparison is between comparable groups |

| **5. Recommendation specific enough to be adopted or rejected** | HSS-IC.B.6 | Calls for further discussion | Recommends a direction | A specific revision the subcommittee could vote on |
| **6. Every project decision appears in the log with criteria written first** | Self-directed learning | Log incomplete or retrospective | Most decisions logged | Every consequential decision, criteria dated before the choice |

## Check on the criteria themselves

**Could Amara meet all ten and still not have learned the standards?**

Criteria 2, 3, and 4 are `HSS-IC.B.3` and `HSS-IC.A.1` and cannot be met without
owning the sampling — which is the gap the profile identifies (she can calculate a
mean; she has never thought about who was in the sample). Criteria 1 and 5 are
`HSS-IC.B.6`. Criterion 4 is the single most important one and the hardest to
fake, because it requires stating what her own work can't do.

The weakest criterion is **5** — a specific recommendation could be produced
without much statistical understanding. It's paired with 4 for that reason.

Criteria 6–10 assess the success skill and are scored separately from the content.
Criterion 10 is the autonomy target: presenting a wrong decision *as information*
rather than as a confession.
