# Learner Profile: Amara

## What Lights Them Up

Judgment. She likes deciding whether something is any good, and she likes having
criteria for it. The teacher spreadsheet is the giveaway — she invented the
criteria, weighted them, and can defend the weighting. She annotates her own
essays arguing with herself in the margins. She reads comment sections not for
the content but to predict the shape of the argument.

So the interest is **evaluation against criteria she constructed.** Not
creativity, not building — assessing. Give her something to judge and a reason to
justify the judgment and she is completely absorbed.

She is also unusually alert to definitional slipperiness. She will stop a
conversation to point out that two people are using "cheating" to mean different
things. That is a genuinely sophisticated move and it's hers, not taught.

## What Gets Hard

**Choosing.** This is the whole thing.

She does exactly what is asked, excellently, and then stops. Given a week to
research freely she produced twelve pages of immaculate notes on the first
subtopic she encountered and never chose a direction — and emailed four times
asking if she was doing it right. When I said it was up to her she said "but what
would you do?" She wasn't fishing. The openness was genuinely distressing.

I think she has learned that instructions are how you avoid being wrong, and a
task without them is a task where she might fail. So she waits, which reads as
diligence and is actually avoidance.

**Starting** anything she hasn't been told the shape of. Once started she is
relentless and finishes on time without chasing.

**Speaking.** She writes far better than she speaks, and under pressure she
retreats into a script and reads it. The content is fine and the delivery is
dead. She isn't frightened of the audience — she's frightened of improvising.

**Applying scepticism symmetrically.** She'll dismantle a badly-sampled statistic
in a news article and accept a well-presented one that agrees with her. Normal,
and worth working on since the project is about evaluating data.

## What We've Already Tried

Assigned work: always excellent, never revealing. She has a folder of A-grade
essays and I cannot tell from any of them what she actually thinks.

The free research week: failed, instructively. My mistake was giving openness
without structure and treating her distress as something she'd push through. She
didn't; she froze productively for a week. The lesson is that the choices need to
be **framed as decisions with criteria**, which is a thing she can do, rather than
as exploration, which is a thing she can't yet.

**Ruled out:** "explore this and see where it goes." Also ruled out: anything with
no criteria available until after she submits. She needs to know what good looks
like — and unlike most learners who say that, she will genuinely use the criteria
rather than gaming them.

One thing to be careful of: she will ask me what I think, repeatedly and
persuasively, and if I answer, the decision becomes mine and the project's whole
purpose evaporates. I need a plan for this, not willpower.
