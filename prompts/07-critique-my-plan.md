# Prompt 7 — Critique my plan

Paste your finished plan and your learner profile, then this. **Ask for this before you launch, not after.**

---

Review this project plan honestly. I'd rather find the problems now than in week
four.

## First, argue against it

**Argue that this project is dessert, not main course** — that the content isn't
really being learned *through* the work. Be specific about which part is weakest.
Then tell me whether you actually believe your own argument.

## Then check the eight elements

For each: is it genuinely present, nominally present, or missing?

1. **Learning goals** — specific standards; ONE success skill with named
   dimensions and a plan for teaching it
2. **Driving question** — open, in my learner's language, and unanswerable
   without the standards
3. **Sustained inquiry** — across all four phases; content not front-loaded
4. **Authenticity** — real context, tools, impact, or a **named** personal
   connection
5. **Voice and choice** — real decisions, calibrated to this learner, widening
6. **Reflection** — twice or more, in different phases, ungraded
7. **Critique and revision** — before the presentation, with a **named person**
   giving it and revision scheduled separately
8. **Public product** — named real people beyond our household

## Then the questions the checklist misses

- **If I deleted my learner's name from this plan, what would still be
  specifically about them?** Quote the parts. If the only answer is "the topic,"
  say so.
- **What have I designed around instead of designing for?** What am I avoiding
  because it's hard for them?
- **Name one thing in this plan that will be hard for my learner and that I'm
  doing anyway.** If there isn't one, I've used what I know about them to lower
  the ceiling — tell me that plainly.
- **Where does this break in week three?** Assume they lose interest and I'm
  tempted to rescue them.
- Is there a scaffold here that will never come off?

## Then

- **Rank the problems** by what would most improve the project. Not in element
  order.
- For each: what's wrong, why it matters concretely, and what to change.
- Tell me what's genuinely good and *why it works*, so I know what to protect
  when I start cutting.
- End with **the single highest-leverage change.**

Don't soften a real problem to be encouraging. A closed driving question is a
closed driving question, and telling me now is kinder than six wasted weeks.
