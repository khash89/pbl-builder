# Prompt 3 — Draft the three foundations

Paste your profile and the chosen idea, then this. **This is the prompt that matters most.**

---

I've chosen a project idea. Now help me get the three foundations right, and let's
not go any further until they are.

## A. Learning goals

- The standards, exactly as I gave them. Do not reword them and do not add any.
- Two "understandings" — what I want them to still understand in a year, in plain
  language, bigger than any single standard.
- **One success skill**, with one or two specific dimensions inside it. One. Not
  two skills, not four. Options: collaboration, critical thinking, creativity,
  complex communication, self-directed learning.
  - Choose it from something I actually described observing, not from a general
    worry.
  - **Don't pick collaboration** unless there's a genuine second person who has
    their own stake and can disagree with my learner. An adult who can overrule
    them isn't a collaborator.
- **How the skill gets taught** — one named routine, used repeatedly, that my
  learner takes over by the end. Not just how it gets assessed.

## B. Driving question

Open-ended, in my learner's language, and answerable only by learning those
standards.

Then test it in front of me and show your reasoning:

1. Could I google the answer? If yes, it's a research prompt — fix it.
2. Does it pose a problem, or name a topic with a question mark on it?
3. **Could my learner produce a decent answer without learning any of my
   standards?** If yes, the standards are decoration — fix it.
4. Would my learner understand it and want to know?

Give me two alternative wordings.

## C. Public product and audience

- Two to four product options that match how this learner likes to make things,
  and are buildable with my actual constraints. For each: what it is, roughly how
  much work, and how exposed my learner is when they hand it over.
- **Name the audience specifically.** "The community" is not an audience. If you
  can't be specific from what I've told you, say what *kind* of audience it needs
  and let me find one.
- If my learner's audience comfort is low, give me a ramp: one trusted adult →
  small friendly group → the real audience.

## Then check the alignment

Show me explicitly:

- Does answering the driving question **require** the standards?
- Does the product **demonstrate** the standards?
- Does the question make the product the natural thing to make?

**Stop there.** Don't plan the weeks yet. If any of the three alignments is weak,
tell me which and fix it first.
