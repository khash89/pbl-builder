# Prompt 6 — Design the assessment

Paste your plan, then this.

---

Help me design the assessment. Two separate things: the content, and the one
success skill.

## Product criteria

Four or five criteria — not twelve — for what makes the product good.

**Derive them from my standards, not from neatness.** If the standard is about
comparing solutions, the criterion is "all three tested options appear, including
the ones that lost" — not "the poster is neat and colourful."

Then apply this test in front of me:

> **Could my learner meet every criterion and still not have learned the
> standard?**

If yes, rewrite the criteria. Also tell me which criterion is the weakest on that
test, so I know which one needs pairing with another.

Write them so my learner can use them alone. "Explains the reasoning" is not
usable. "Can explain the three-step chain without notes" is.

## Formative assessment

For each of the two middle phases, at least one check. For each one:

- What I'd look at
- When
- **What I would actually DO if it showed a problem.** If you can't answer that,
  drop the check — assessment I don't act on is just record-keeping.

Then: **one observable thing I could count weekly** that tracks the success
skill. A number I track beats an impression I form.

## The success skill

- How to check the same thing at the start and the end — same task, same prompt,
  so the comparison means something
- A self-assessment my learner does **before** I say anything
- Assessed separately from the content

## And tell me

- Where I'm most likely to assess effort instead of the work, because I watched
  the effort. That's the hard part of assessing your own kid.
- Whether there's anyone outside our household who could look at the final
  product. In a one-learner setting an outside reading is the only real check
  available.
