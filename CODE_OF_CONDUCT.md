# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want this to be a project where teachers, parents, tutors, researchers, and developers can work together productively. We pledge to make participation a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Standards

**Behavior that helps:**

- Assuming the person on the other end knows something you don't. A homeschool parent, a special-education teacher, and a TypeScript developer all have expertise here, and it isn't the same expertise.
- Giving feedback about the work, specifically, with a suggested alternative.
- Accepting correction gracefully. Practitioner experience beats author intent — if someone says a strategy doesn't work the way we described it, that's useful information, not an attack.
- Being patient with people who are new to PBL, to git, or to both.

**Behavior that doesn't:**

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, public or private
- Sexualized language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention
- Publishing others' private information without permission
- **Posting identifiable information about a child.** See below — this is the one specific to this project.
- Condescension toward contributors based on their technical or pedagogical background
- Sustained disruption of discussions

## Child privacy

This project is about designing learning for specific children, which means contributors will be tempted to bring real examples. Don't.

Do not post, in issues, pull requests, discussions, or commits:

- A child's full name, school, or town
- Photos of children or of student work that identifies them
- Diagnoses, IEP or 504 contents, assessment scores, or medical information
- A learner profile describing a real, identifiable child

Use invented learners or thoroughly de-identified ones. See [`learners/README.md`](learners/README.md).

Content that identifies a child will be removed promptly, without waiting for the poster's agreement. This isn't punitive — it's that the child has no say in the matter and can't undo it later.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code comments — and when representing the project publicly.

## Enforcement

Report unacceptable behavior by [opening an issue](https://github.com/khash89/pbl-builder/issues) if it's public and minor, or by contacting the maintainers privately for anything sensitive. Reports involving child privacy or harassment should go through the private channel.

All reports will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. Maintainers will respect the privacy and security of the reporter.

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this code in good faith may face temporary or permanent repercussions as determined by other maintainers.

### Consequences

1. **Correction** — a private warning explaining what was wrong and why
2. **Warning** — a warning with consequences for continued behavior, including a period of no interaction with the people involved
3. **Temporary ban** — from any interaction with the project
4. **Permanent ban** — for sustained violations, harassment, or aggression toward individuals or groups

Child-privacy violations skip straight to content removal, then follow the ladder above depending on intent and response.
