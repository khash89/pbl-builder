# Letter to Family

*For grandparents, a co-parent, a co-op, or anyone who'll be asked "what are
you working on?" Adapt freely.*

---

Dear [name],

[Learner] is starting a project that will run for about [N] weeks, and I wanted
to tell you about it — partly because you'll hear a lot about it, and partly
because we may need your help.

**The question they're working on:**

> [Driving question]

**What they'll make:** [product], which they'll present to [audience] on
[date].

**What they'll learn:** [one or two sentences in plain language — what they'll
be able to do that they can't yet].

**Why we're doing it this way.** Rather than reading about [topic] and then
being tested on it, [learner] will learn it by actually working on [the real
problem]. It's slower than a textbook chapter and it sticks a great deal
better — mostly because there's a real reason to get it right, and real people
who'll see the result.

**How you can help:**

- **Ask them about it** — and ask hard questions, not just encouraging ones.
  "How do you know that?" is the most useful thing you can say.
- **Come to the presentation** on [date] if you can.
- [Specific ask — be a critique audience in week 3, help with transport, put
  them in touch with someone]

**One thing that might look odd.** They'll produce a draft that isn't very good,
and we'll deliberately leave it that way for a while before revising. That's the
point — the work improves through feedback rather than being right first time.
If they show you something rough, being honest about what isn't clear yet helps
much more than saying it's lovely.

Thank you,

[Your name]

---
