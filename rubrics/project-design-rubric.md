# Project Design Rubric

**Use this before you launch.** It checks the design of the project, on paper, while changes are still cheap.

`pbl review` automates about half of what's here — the mechanical half. This rubric covers the judgments no checker can make.

Three levels: **Not yet** · **Developing** · **Gold Standard**. Score honestly; a plan that scores Developing on three elements is a normal first project, not a failure.

---

## 1. Key Knowledge, Understanding & Success Skills

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Standards were chosen after the activity was designed. Too many to teach properly. Success skills are named but not taught. | Two to four standards, genuinely served by the project. One success skill named with dimensions, but `taught_how` is thin. | Two to four standards, each with named evidence. One skill, 1–3 dimensions, with a specific routine for teaching it that the learner takes over. |

**Ask yourself:** could the learner meet every product criterion and still not have learned the standard?

---

## 2. Challenging Problem or Question

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Googleable, or a topic with a question mark. Written in curriculum language. | Open-ended and in the learner's language, but answering it doesn't strictly require the standards. | Open, in their words, genuinely contested, and unanswerable without the target content. The learner is implicated in it. |

**Ask yourself:** could they produce a decent answer without learning any of my standards?

---

## 3. Sustained Inquiry

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Content front-loaded before the project. Inquiry is one research session. | Inquiry in the middle phases. Some learner questions drive instruction. | Inquiry across all four phases. Their need-to-knows sequence the instruction. Multiple modes — reading, doing, asking people. |

**Ask yourself:** if their questions turn out different from what I anticipated, does my plan bend or break?

---

## 4. Authenticity

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| "Authentic" means a career is mentioned. Personal connection asserted, not named. | A real context and real processes. Personal connection names an interest. | Real context, real tools and quality standards, honest account of impact, and a personal connection that names a specific interest from the profile. |

**Ask yourself:** whose actual job does this resemble? And if impact is honestly nothing, did I say so rather than inventing it?

---

## 5. Learner Voice & Choice

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Choices are trivial — format, colour, title. Or an open field handed to a learner who freezes. | Real choices in one or two categories. Calibrated to their appetite. | Real choices across content, process, and product; calibrated to appetite and experience; and they widen during the project on a named trigger. |

**Ask yourself:** is any of this a choice I've already made and intend to steer them toward?

---

## 6. Reflection

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| One reflection at the end. Or reflections are graded. | Two or more reflection points across phases, mostly on content. | Reflection on content, process, *and* purpose, at multiple points, ungraded, with the final one deliberately delayed. |

**Ask yourself:** am I asking about how the work is going, or only about what they learned?

---

## 7. Critique & Revision

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Feedback arrives with the grade. Or no critique partner is named, so it won't happen. | One critique cycle before presenting, with named partners. | Critique against criteria, from more than one voice including someone who isn't you, with revision scheduled as its own block, and the learner choosing what to act on. |

**Ask yourself:** who specifically is giving this feedback, and is revision on the calendar as its own session?

---

## 8. Public Product

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Audience is the household, or "the community," or was dropped because the learner is anxious. | A specific real audience beyond the household. | Named real people who aren't obliged to be interested; the learner explains their reasoning; a comfort ramp if needed; a backup that is still public. |

**Ask yourself:** can I name the people who will be in the room?

---

## Personalization — specific to this repo

Not in the PBLWorks framework. It's the point of this one.

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Remove the learner's name and nothing in the plan identifies who it's for. | The topic reflects their interests; scaffolds address their needs. | Topic, product, scaffolds, pacing, choice level, and audience approach are all traceable to specific profile fields. Every need has a scaffold; every scaffold has a fade trigger. |

**Ask yourself — the single most useful question on this page:** if I deleted the learner's name from this plan, what would still be specific to them? Quote the parts.

---

## Feasibility

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Depends on resources you don't have. No slack. No stuck protocol. | Achievable with the actual constraints. Some slack. | Built on real resources; slack in the calendar; a real stuck protocol; and a `when_to_stop` tripwire decided in advance. |

**Ask yourself:** what happens when week three goes badly? Is the answer written down?

---

## The two questions that matter most

**1. Is this dessert?**
If you removed the project, would the learner have learned the same content anyway? If yes, the content isn't being learned *through* the work.

**2. Would this be recognisably for this learner if you took the name off?**
If not, you've designed a good project and attached a child to it, rather than the other way round.

---

## Scoring

Count your Gold Standards. But the useful output isn't a number — it's:

- **The lowest-scoring element.** Fix that one. It's usually critique or public product.
- **Anything at Not yet.** Those are structural and won't fix themselves during the project.
- **One thing you'll deliberately leave at Developing.** First projects can't be Gold Standard everywhere. Choosing which corner to cut, consciously, beats discovering it in week four.

Then run:

```bash
pbl review path/to/project-plan.md
```

And after the project, come back with the [teaching rubric](project-based-teaching-rubric.md) — because a well-designed project can still be under-run, and that's the more common failure.
