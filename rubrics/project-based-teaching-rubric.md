# Project Based Teaching Rubric

**Use this after the project, on yourself.**

The [design rubric](project-design-rubric.md) checks the plan. This checks how you ran it — and most disappointing projects are well-designed and under-run, so this is usually the more useful document.

Nobody else will score this. That's the point, and it's why honesty here is worth more than a good result.

---

## 1. Design & Plan

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Planned as you went. Resources arranged late or not at all. | A real plan; some resources arranged in advance; the calendar mostly held. | All eight elements present; experts and audience confirmed before week one; the plan bent where you expected it to. |

**Honest question:** what did you arrange in week two that should have been arranged in week zero?

---

## 2. Align to Standards

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Criteria were about neatness and completion. | Criteria derived from standards, referred to at the end. | Criteria from standards, referred to throughout, and the learner could say what the standard was asking for. |

**Honest question:** did the learner ever know which standard the work was serving?

---

## 3. Build the Culture

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Norms were your rules. Revision felt like punishment. Wrong answers were quietly corrected. | Norms agreed. Revision normal. Some risk-taking. | Norms co-crafted and self-monitored. Being wrong treated as information by both of you. The learner took a risk you didn't suggest. |

**Honest question:** did you ever say out loud that a draft isn't good yet *without* the learner hearing that they aren't good at it? Did you check?

---

## 4. Manage Activities

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| The schedule lived in your head. You reminded them what to do next most days. | A visible calendar; the learner tracked some of it. | The learner held the calendar and the project wall by the middle of the project, and knew what to do next without asking. |

**Honest question:** by the last week, who was driving the schedule?

---

## 5. Scaffold Learning

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Supports front-loaded before they were needed. Nothing faded. | Scaffolds responsive; some faded. | Each scaffold introduced at the point of need and faded on its trigger. The success skill was explicitly taught, practised, and handed over. |

**Honest question:** which scaffold is still in place that should have come off? Be specific.

---

## 6. Assess Learning

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Judged the final product. Formative assessment happened but didn't change anything. | Formative assessment changed the plan at least once. Learner self-assessed with prompting. | Multiple sources of evidence; formative assessment routinely changed what you did; the learner self-assessed *first*; the success skill assessed separately; at least one outside voice on the final product. |

**Honest questions:** name one time you changed the plan because of something you noticed. And: did you score against the written criteria, or against the effort you watched?

---

## 7. Engage & Coach

| Not yet | Developing | Gold Standard |
|---|---|---|
| You solved problems for them. Expectations quietly lowered when it got hard. | Mostly coached rather than rescued. Expectations held. | Their questions drove the inquiry; you named which role you were in; expectations held under pressure; you reflected formally too. |

**Honest question:** how many times did you fix something because it would have taken you ten seconds?

---

## The two things to be honest about

### Rescuing

Count them. Every time you did the thing instead of letting them struggle through it.

Nobody scores zero, and zero would be its own problem. But the count tells you something, and it's the single most useful number to come out of a solo project. If it's high, the [stuck ladder](../framework/scaffolds.yaml) is the intervention — it gives you something to point at instead of solving.

### Pushing

Did you carry the project on your own enthusiasm at any point?

The tell: a week where you were more invested than they were and you pushed through it anyway. Sometimes right. Often the beginning of the project becoming yours.

If you wrote a `when_to_stop` tripwire, did you hit it? Did you honour it?

---

## Then do the useful thing

Scoring yourself is not the output. These three are:

**1. One practice to work on next project.** One. Named specifically — "I will not answer a question in the first thirty seconds" beats "scaffold better."

**2. The plan's Notes & Adaptations section, written now.** What changed, what surprised you, where it stalled, what you'd cut. In three months you won't remember why week two went badly.

**3. The learner profile, updated.** → [post-project reflection](../docs/10-strategies/post-project-reflection.md#then-update-the-profile)

That third one is what makes the next project better, and it's the step almost everyone skips.

---

## And ask them

The learner's answer to *"how could I have made this better for you?"* will tell you more than this rubric will.

Ask it two days after the presentation, not the same evening. Walking, not across a table. Then don't defend the answer.
