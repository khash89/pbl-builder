# Collaboration

> Working with other people to produce something none of you would have produced alone — combining thinking, negotiating disagreement, and sharing responsibility for the result.

**Pick one or two dimensions from this rubric — not all of them.** Everything you do not name is explicitly out of scope for this project, and saying so out loud is part of keeping the focus real.

---

> ### ⚠ Before targeting this skill with one learner
>
> Collaboration is the one success skill that cannot be faked in a solo project. If there is no genuine second party with their own stake and their own opinions, do not target this skill — you will end up assessing compliance with an adult, which is a different thing entirely and teaches the wrong lesson.
>
> Target it only if at least one of these is genuinely true:
>
> - A co-op, sibling, or partner learner is a real collaborator with shared ownership of the product and the right to disagree
> - The learner works with an outside expert or community partner across several exchanges, not a single interview
> - The learner is collaborating with a client whose requirements they don't control
>
> An adult who can overrule the learner is not a collaborator. If none of the above holds, pick complex-communication or self-directed-learning instead — both are genuinely assessable alone, and both transfer.

## Shared Planning & Decision-Making

`planning-and-decisions` — *How do they organise the work together?*

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Beginning** | Divides work by convenience or seniority, or does it all themselves. Inflexible once the split is made. Doesn't consider who is suited to what. |
| **Emerging** | Participates in dividing the work and mostly holds to the agreement. Division tends to be equal-sized rather than well-matched. |
| **Developing** | Suggests a division based on what people are actually good at and interested in. Renegotiates when circumstances change. |
| **Demonstrating** | Proposes roles that draw on each person's specific strengths, identifies the trade-offs between different ways of proceeding, and suggests how the group should decide when it disagrees — before it has to. |

**How to teach it**

- Require a written working agreement with a named process for disagreement
- Ask who is best placed to do this and why, rather than who wants to
- Debrief the process separately from the product

**What tells you it's working:** Proposing a decision-making rule before a decision is contested. Genuinely sophisticated and rare.

## Building on Others' Thinking

`building-on-thinking` — *Do they use what other people say?*

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Beginning** | Restates or repeats what others said. Contributions sit side by side without connecting. |
| **Emerging** | Responds to others' ideas and agrees or disagrees. Builds on them occasionally. |
| **Developing** | Extends others' ideas with new information, and can summarise where the group has got to. |
| **Demonstrating** | Rephrases the group's collective thinking, connects it to new information, and names what's still missing — moving the group forward rather than adding to a pile. |

**How to teach it**

- "What I heard was ___, and it makes me think ___" as a required frame
- Assign someone to summarise where the group stands every ten minutes
- Ban starting a turn without referencing the previous one

**What tells you it's working:** Naming a gap in the group's shared thinking. That's the demonstrating behaviour.

## Productive Disagreement

`productive-disagreement` — *What happens when they don't agree?*

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Beginning** | Withdraws, gives in immediately, or escalates. Disagreement becomes about the people. |
| **Emerging** | Stays in the conversation but disagreement stalls — either an impasse or a coin-flip. |
| **Developing** | Keeps disagreement on the substance. Looks for what both positions assume and where they overlap. |
| **Demonstrating** | Frames disagreement so it's productive — states the other position fairly, identifies the real point of difference, and helps the group find common ground or a legitimate way to decide without one. |

**How to teach it**

- Require stating the other position to its holder's satisfaction first
- "What would change your mind?" asked of everyone including themselves
- Separate the disagreement from the decision — explore, then decide

**What tells you it's working:** Improving the argument they disagree with. Almost unmistakable evidence.

## Shared Responsibility

`shared-responsibility` — *Do they carry their share and support the others?*

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Beginning** | Does their piece and nothing more, or takes over everything. Missed commitments become someone else's problem. |
| **Emerging** | Completes their own commitments with reminders. Aware of others' progress but not acting on it. |
| **Developing** | Reliable, and notices when a teammate is stuck. Offers help without taking over. |
| **Demonstrating** | Holds themselves accountable to the group, raises problems early rather than at the deadline, and supports teammates in a way that builds their capability instead of substituting for it. |

**How to teach it**

- A shared, visible task log — everyone sees everyone's status
- Normalise raising a slip early; make it explicitly safe
- "Help without doing it for them" as an explicit, practised skill

**What tells you it's working:** Flagging their own slip before anyone notices. That's the behaviour that makes teams work.

## What the project has to supply

This skill cannot grow without these. If the project does not provide them,
change the project or change the skill.

- A genuine second party with their own stake and the standing to disagree
- Work that actually requires more than one person — not divisible into parallel solo tasks
- A written working agreement, including how disagreements get settled
- Process debriefs separate from product feedback

**Products that suit it:** `event`, `business-or-venture-plan`, `documentary-or-video`, `exhibit-or-installation`, `interactive-map-or-tour` — see [product-types.yaml](../../framework/product-types.yaml).

**Where it goes wrong:** A product that splits cleanly into independent parts produces cooperation, not collaboration — everyone does their bit and it gets stapled together. The work has to require joint thinking. See solo_warning above before choosing this skill at all in a one-learner setting.

## Using it

- **The learner self-assesses first**, in their own words, before you say anything.
  The gap between their judgement and yours is the most useful data in the project.
- **Same task, twice** — first week and last week, on the same prompt, so the
  comparison means something. Show them both.
- **One level of growth in a project is a real result.** Two is a lot.
- **Assess it separately from the content.** A learner can produce an excellent
  product with weak skill, and the reverse.
